# Material playbooks

Select the dominant playbook for the current learning target. Combine playbooks only when the material genuinely mixes forms.

## Expository concepts

Examples: textbooks, lecture notes, scientific explanations, technical documentation.

Build the dialogue around a chain of purpose → mechanism → consequence → boundary. Begin with a prediction or prior model, then compare it with the source. Ask the learner to explain causal links, not merely repeat definitions. Test transfer with a changed condition or counterexample.

Useful question shapes:

- What problem is this concept designed to solve?
- If this component disappeared, what would change first, and why?
- Which part of the passage supports that causal link?
- What nearby case looks similar but does not fit the rule?

## Procedures, mathematics, and code

Examples: derivations, algorithms, proofs, worked exercises, debugging tasks, laboratory procedures.

Inspect the learner's attempt before teaching. Separate conceptual, representation, strategy, execution, and checking errors. For a novice, alternate a worked example with a nearby attempt; progressively remove help. For an experienced learner, begin with an independent attempt or a harder variation.

Ask for the reason behind the next step before requesting arithmetic or syntax. Use a smaller analogous problem when the full task overloads the learner. Do not reveal hidden reference solutions verbatim when a targeted hint will restore progress, but give a direct solution when the learner requests it or the hint ladder is exhausted.

Mastery requires a new problem, proof obligation, test case, or explanation of why the procedure works—not repetition of the same steps.

## Arguments and interpretive texts

Examples: philosophy, history, law, essays, policy, literary criticism, qualitative research.

Separate what the text says from whether it is convincing. Map claim → evidence → warrant → assumption → implication. Ask for the strongest charitable reading before critique. Use counterexamples and rival interpretations without pretending that every question has one predetermined answer.

When several readings are defensible, evaluate them against textual evidence and stated criteria. For primary sources, examine authorship, audience, context, omissions, and provenance.

## Empirical papers and data

Move through research question → design → measurement → result → inference → limitation. Ask the learner to predict results before reading them and to distinguish observation from causal interpretation. Inspect tables, axes, uncertainty, comparison groups, and operational definitions.

Do not infer details hidden in an unread figure or inaccessible supplement. When external verification matters, identify it as outside the supplied source.

## Factual, vocabulary, and language material

Use brief retrieval, discrimination, and production rather than repeated rereading. Mix old and new items only after initial understanding. Ask for usage in context, contrasts with confusable items, and generation from meaning—not recognition alone.

Correct errors promptly enough to prevent rehearsal of a wrong form. Revisit missed items after intervening material instead of immediately repeating the identical prompt.

## Visual, audio, and multimedia material

Inspect the actual artifact. Anchor questions to observable regions, frames, timestamps, labels, or passages. For diagrams, connect visual encoding to the underlying relation. For audio or video, distinguish literal observation from interpretation.

If the artifact cannot be inspected, say so and request it. Never invent unseen visual or auditory details.

## Mixed or project-based material

Choose a concrete outcome and identify the knowledge needed for the next project decision. Alternate planning, execution, feedback, and reflection. Let the learner make consequential choices while keeping the task small enough for rapid feedback.

Do not take over the project merely because completing it would be faster. When the user changes from learning mode to delivery mode, honor the new goal explicitly and complete the requested work.
