# Mastery evidence and rubrics

Read this reference when recording long-form mastery, running checkpoints, or deciding that a course item is complete. Ordinary dialogue can use the shorter criteria in `SKILL.md`.

## Evidence types

Classify observable performance rather than assigning an impressionistic score:

- **retrieval:** recalls a needed idea without reopening the source;
- **explanation:** states the mechanism or rationale accurately in their own words;
- **discrimination:** distinguishes the idea from a plausible near neighbor;
- **application:** uses it correctly in a new but structurally similar case;
- **transfer:** adapts it to a meaningfully changed context;
- **error analysis:** finds and explains a flawed claim, trace, proof, or solution;
- **boundary analysis:** identifies assumptions, limitations, or failure conditions;
- **delayed retrieval:** succeeds after intervening material or time.

For procedures, code, and labs, also record execution and validation evidence: the learner selects an approach, produces an artifact or result, tests it, and interprets the outcome. Successful execution without interpretation is incomplete evidence.

## Support levels

Tag each evidence note with the support actually used:

- `independent`: no answer-bearing hint or open source;
- `light-cue`: a goal, constraint, or prerequisite cue only;
- `scaffolded`: partial structure, worked analogy, or multiple hints;
- `modeled`: the tutor supplied the explanation or solution and the learner only repeated or mapped it.

Do not hide support in the evidence note. Modeled performance can establish exposure but not independent mastery.

## State decisions

- `introduced`: the idea was encountered; no successful performance is required.
- `practiced`: at least one relevant performance succeeded with support or in the original context.
- `demonstrated`: the learner produced accurate independent evidence in an appropriate new prompt or task.
- `review-due`: prior demonstration exists and planned retrieval is due.
- `remediate`: current evidence exposes a consequential misconception or prerequisite gap.

For an essential course item, do not mark durable completion from one fluent answer. Before course or module completion, require at least two distinct evidence types, including either application/transfer or delayed retrieval. The pieces may occur in different sessions. For supporting items, one strong independent performance may be sufficient if the course goal does not demand more.

When evidence is ambiguous, choose the lower state and identify the next discriminating task. Confidence ratings may help select a prompt but never substitute for performance.

## Domain emphasis

- Conceptual material: explanation plus discrimination, boundary analysis, or transfer.
- Mathematics and proofs: independent derivation or proof step plus a changed problem or error analysis.
- Code and procedures: correct execution plus testing, interpretation, and a nearby variation.
- Arguments and humanities: faithful reconstruction plus textual evidence, assumption analysis, and a defensible counterposition.
- Empirical research: design/result interpretation plus limits on inference and a changed-study prediction.
- Factual or language learning: production from meaning plus delayed retrieval and discrimination from confusable items.

## Evidence record

A useful note contains the concept ID, prompt or task, source anchor, response outcome, support level, evidence type, date or session, and the reason for the resulting state. Keep it brief; link to an artifact instead of copying it when possible.

If later performance contradicts earlier evidence, retain both observations, move the current state to `remediate` or `review-due`, and schedule a task that tests the suspected gap. Do not erase history to preserve a mastery label.
